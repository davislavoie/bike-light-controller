Build notes

  • Common ground. Battery −, regulator GND, KB2040 GND, every MOSFET source and the HUD LED cathodes all tie together.
  • Headlight driver (Q1). IRLB3813PbF (TO-220, logic-level, legs G-D-S), low-side: GP6 → 22Ω → gate, 10k gate → GND, source → GND, drain → headlight −, headlight + → 12V. PWM at 25kHz (above hearing; lower rates made the board's ceramic caps whine) gives LOW 5% / MED 25% / HIGH 100%. STROBE has three patterns: steady + pulse (steady → pulse: 5→25% on LOW, 25→50% on MED, 50→100% on HIGH, pulse 0.1s every second), daytime flasher (80ms flash every 0.75s, dark between) and double flash (two quick flashes once a second). Flick SW4 into STROBE twice within 1s to step to the next; the choice is saved to flash and survives power-off. The 22Ω resistor and 12mA pin drive keep switching fast enough that the TO-220 only gets warm; check by touch at MED.
  • Rear lamp drivers (U3–U5). The lamp has one shared ground, so brake and turns are switched on the 12V side. Each is a Toshiba TLP241A photorelay (DIP-4, 40V / 2A): GPIO → 330Ω → pin 1, pin 2 → GND lights its LED, and the isolated output (pins 3/4) connects 12V to the lamp wire like a jumper. Set GP7–GP9 to 8mA drive in firmware. Up to 5ms switching, which is fine because these are on/off only, never PWM: the lamp's own electronics run the breathing, brake flash and sequential turn sweep. The firmware holds a turn output steady on while that blinker (or hazards) is active; the HUD turn LED blinks ~1.5Hz on its own timer. With the headlight on a daytime strobe (daytime flasher or double flash), the brake output flashes in step with it (plain on/off, no PWM); a real brake overrides that with solid on.
  • Wiring and connectors. Battery, master switch and 5A fuse on the cross tube; Wago lever nuts split 12V and GND. The rear lamp's white (tail) and black (GND) go straight to the Wagos, so the tail is on whenever the master switch is. One harness runs up to the handlebar box on J1, Nano-Fit 2×3: 1 +12V · 2 GND · 3 brake (red) · 4 turn (yellow) · 5 turn (green) · 6 GND. The headlight has its own J2, Nano-Fit 1×2; the different pin counts mean they can't be swapped. 22 AWG for power, 24 AWG for signals. The listing doesn't say which of yellow/green is left; touch each to 12V before crimping.
  • Brake levers. NC switch from GPIO to GND, using the RP2040's internal pull-up (INPUT_PULLUP); firmware debounces it. At rest the pin reads LOW; a squeezed lever or a cut wire reads HIGH, so a broken wire fails safe with the brake light on.
  • Lever gestures. Both levers squeezed → brake light at once. One lever held past the double-tap window → brake light. A tap is a squeeze under 250ms; a second tap within 400ms of the first is a double-tap (both are tunable constants). Double-tap one lever → toggle that blinker (manual cancel only; tapping the other side switches over). Double-tap both levers → toggle hazards; single-side taps are ignored while hazards are on, and braking still works.
  • Headlight switch. ON-OFF-ON toggle: common → GND, throws → GP4 / GP5 with internal pull-ups. Both HIGH means off.
  • Brightness switch. ON-OFF-ON toggle: common → GND, throws → GP26 (LOW) / GP28 (HIGH) with internal pull-ups; centre = MED. It only dims the headlight. Brake and turn signals always run at full brightness so drivers can see them.
  • HUD dimming (firmware). The turn and battery LEDs are PWM-dimmed (keep the 330Ω resistors). Daytime (headlight off, or the daytime flasher / double flash strobes): full brightness. Night (headlight ON, or the steady + pulse strobe): 60% / 30% / 10% with the brightness switch at LOW / MED / HIGH (HUD_DUTY in BikeLogic.h).
  • Battery bar and cutoff (firmware). A1 reads VBAT × 22/122, averaged, with ~0.1V hysteresis. 4 bars ≥11.7V · 3 bars 11.1–11.7V · 2 bars 10.6–11.1V · 1 bar 10.0–10.6V · 1 bar blinking below 10.0V (the rear lamp shuts itself off there). At 9.4V held for 5s every light goes off and the RP2040 sleeps (~2–3mA); turn the master switch off. With 5% divider resistors, calibrate once against a multimeter (or use 1% parts).
  • Regulator. Mornsun K7805W-500R3: 6.5–36V in, 5V 0.5A switching module with the LM7805 pinout (1 VIN, 2 GND, 3 VOUT). It has plenty of headroom for the KB2040 and HUD LEDs, which draw well under 100mA.
